If you’ll give me 5 minutes of your time, I truly believe we can save lives and change this country for the better, together.

Before we get started, a couple of quick notes so we’re on the same page:

- When I talk about “you”, I’m describing _The Average American Who Works For A Living And Gets Health Insurance Through Their Job_. Your own individual situation may differ from examples I use below, and I ask for your grace and understanding that I, of course, can’t make this perfectly unique for every person. If something does not apply to you directly, please imagine _The Average American_ to whom it would.
- When I say “Medicare for All” or “MfA”, I’m referring to a healthcare system where services like doctor visits and medications are paid for by the government, from money raised by taxes. There are a bunch of ideas with the same basic premise (government provides healthcare) so I’m just going to use MfA as a stand-in for all of them.

Okay! Now let’s start with something I bet we’ll agree on:

# Life is a human right.

“…endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights, that among these are **Life**, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness.”

Life, an unalienable right, the American way since 1776.

1. Life is a gift, a **right**, that everyone is born with. It’s not something a king, ruler, or government allows you to have.
2. Life is right that cannot be sold, given away, or taken away by any law or government. It is **unalienable**.

Okay. This makes sense to me. If we can agree that life is a human right—and I sincerely hope we can—let’s take another step.

# What the heck is the government even for?

Obviously, this is a very complex question and a lot of people have been thinking about this for thousands of years. For the sake of this website, and because I’m definitely not an political science major, I’ll keep it basic:

**The purpose of a government is to maintain order, protect individual rights, provide essential public services, and promote the general welfare of its citizens.**

Accurate enough, I hope?

So if we put these two points together, we end up with:
1. Life is a human right.
2. The purpose of government is to protect the rights of its citizens.

Therefore, the government should provide healthcare to everyone! You’re convinced! We did it! Thanks for reading. :)

Okay, okay, I know, it’s not that simple. You probably have some thoughts and questions. Let’s talk about the most common ones.

#Cool idea, but how will we pay for it?

This is the big one. And I’ll be honest, it’s true: your taxes will go up. Increasing taxes on individuals and employers, along with fairly taxing huge corporations and billionaires, will be required to pay for this.

## BUT

That question is actually a trick. It’s only half of the math, and the for-profit insurance companies don’t want you to think about that. Your taxes will go up, yes, but consider what you’re paying for today. Insurance, co-pays, prescriptions, deductibles. All of that disappears under Medicare for All. 

In fact, did you know America currently has the most expensive healthcare system in the world? We actually pay more—a lot more—for our healthcare than any other wealthy nation. You pay around $16,500 every year for healthcare. But under Medicare for All, with tax increases, you’ll pay somewhere around $11,500–$14,000.

You will pay **less** for Medicare for All. A lot less. Like **$2,500–$5000 less, every year.**

# But I like my insurance plan!

First off: do you _actually_ like it, or is it just better than other plans? Because under Medicare for All, everyone gets better care, and for lower costs. That includes you!

And even if you do like your insurance plan for some reason, did you know healthcare in America is actually…bad? Like _very_ bad? Like “the worst care of any developed nation” bad? 

Life expectancy in the US is worse than Australia, Canada, France, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Sweden, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom. The US has the most barriers to accessing healthcare, and the highest rate of preventable and treatable deaths for all ages. It’s even difficult for providers! Our administrative efficiency is the second-worst of any country in that list.

We’re paying more than everyone else, and we’re getting less than everyone else.

#I heard people in (insert country here) have to wait months to see a doctor.

This kind of rumor has been going around for years. It’s not true.

For a basic doctor’s appointment (PCP or GP), on average:
- USA: 21 days
- Italy: 10 days
- UK: 10 days
- Sweden: 7 days
- Australia: 4 days
- Switzerland: 2 days

And remember, they’re all receiving better care than we are. And it costs them less, too.

#And that’s not all!

Beyond the direct cost of healthcare, think about all the other problems that for-profit healthcare causes in America:

Medical debt in the US:
- Today: $220 billion, over 100 million adults
- Medicare for All: **zero**

Families filing for bankruptcy because of crushing medical debt:
- Today: 500,000+ every year
- Medicare for All: **zero**.

Adults with medical debt ($500 or more) affecting their credit score:
- Today: 15 million+
- Medicare for All: **zero**

People dying prematurely because they don’t have health insurance:
- Today: 20,000–60,000 every year
- Medicare for All: **zero**

Remember how we’re paying the most for healthcare? For-profit insurance companies spend 12-19% of that on administrative overhead and marketing. Your money going towards paperwork, executive salaries, red tape, corporate shenanigans.

Medicare for All? Around 2% for overhead. That’s right, you never thought you’d see the day, but it’s true: the government is actually _way more efficient_.

#The bottom line.
America, today, is paying more money for healthcare than any other country, and getting worse care.

We could be spending less, for better care, for more us.
